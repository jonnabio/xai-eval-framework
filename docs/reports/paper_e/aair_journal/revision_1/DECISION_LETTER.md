## Reviewer-1

**1. Comments to Editor:**
The manuscript is methodologically careful and addresses a relevant problem in explainable AI. I consider the use of instance-paired SHAP and LIME explanations, run-level statistical summaries, seed-clustered resampling, pre-specification of the main analysis, transparent post-hoc labeling, and complete release of code and result files to be notable strengths. I do not identify a fundamental methodological problem requiring major revision. The principal remaining issues concern the scope of interpretation. In the Adult cohort, a substantial proportion of explained instances for four seeds belong to the training data of the single fitted model, and the primary LIME configuration uses a kernel width of 3 selected without a technical rationale. The manuscript itself demonstrates that kernel width can materially change LIME's selected feature lists. These limitations are already acknowledged, but I recommend that the authors strengthen the held-out sensitivity assessment and further bound the main conclusions to the exact explainer configurations evaluated. The SVM analysis is also based on incomplete SHAP coverage, which should continue to be treated cautiously. Overall, these issues appear addressable without a redesign of the study or major new experimentation. I therefore recommend **Minor Revision**.

**2. Comments to Author:**
The manuscript presents a careful and potentially valuable empirical investigation of instance-level agreement between SHAP and LIME. Important strengths include the use of paired explanations for the same instances and models, run-level rather than instance-level statistical summaries, explicit treatment of the dependence structure, pre-specification of the main analysis, transparent identification of post-hoc analyses, and extensive reproducibility materials. The manuscript is generally well written and the conclusions are appropriately cautious. I have only a few points that should be addressed before publication. 1. The Adult analysis would benefit from a clearer sensitivity assessment restricted to held-out instances for the primary agreement measures. The manuscript states that, for four of the five Adult seeds, approximately 70% of the paired instances are also rows used to train the fitted model. A held-out-only sensitivity analysis is already reported for the correct-versus-misclassified comparison, which is useful. However, the main RQ1/RQ2 agreement estimates are still largely based on the combined training and held-out instances. Please either provide a corresponding held-out-only sensitivity analysis for the principal top-k agreement estimates or more explicitly justify why training-set membership is not expected to materially affect these conclusions. 2. The dependence of the findings on the LIME configuration should be emphasized more prominently. The primary analyses use a fixed kernel width of 3, which the manuscript acknowledges was selected without a technical justification and is smaller than the package default. The post-hoc experiment shows that changing the kernel width can alter the feature lists substantially; on Adult, the overlap between LIME at width 3 and LIME at the default width is itself only 0.370. This is an important finding. The Abstract and Conclusion should therefore make particularly clear that the reported SHAP–LIME agreement levels are conditional on the evaluated LIME configuration rather than intrinsic properties of the two explainer families. 3. Please consider whether the principal self-agreement result should be described even more explicitly as a sensitivity/reference analysis rather than as a general property of SHAP and LIME. The experiment is based on a relatively small subsample and one pair of random states, as appropriately acknowledged in the Limitations section. This does not invalidate the result, but the language used in the Abstract and Conclusion should remain consistent with this limited scope. 4. The manuscript appropriately identifies that LIME without discretisation returns local slopes whereas SHAP returns contributions. The post-hoc conversion analysis is informative and substantially strengthens the paper. However, I suggest making the distinction between the pre-specified sign analysis and the post-hoc corrected interpretation especially visible in the Discussion, since the original sign-agreement metric does not compare equivalent quantities. 5. The SVM results should remain explicitly qualified wherever model-family comparisons are discussed. The manuscript reports that usable SHAP explanations are available for only 2,064 of 7,000 LIME-explained SVM instances (29.5%) and that this subset is not balanced across predicted classes. The current manuscript acknowledges this limitation, but it would be useful to ensure that no broad model-family ranking is inferred from the SVM estimates. These points do not require a fundamental redesign of the study. The main empirical contribution—the demonstration that agreement between SHAP and LIME is partial, configuration-dependent, and generally lower than within-method self-agreement in the evaluated settings—is supported by the presented analyses. I therefore recommend Minor Revision.

**Comments and Suggestions for Author:** The manuscript is generally rigorous, transparent, and reproducible. Before publication, I recommend strengthening the sensitivity analysis or interpretation of the Adult results with respect to training-set membership and further emphasizing that the reported SHAP–LIME agreement values depend on the specific LIME configuration, particularly the fixed kernel width of 3. The self-agreement and sign-conversion analyses should also continue to be clearly identified as post-hoc sensitivity/reference analyses, and the incomplete SVM coverage should remain explicitly qualified. These are limited revisions and do not alter the main contribution of the study.

**Recommendation:** Minor Revision

## Reviewer-2

**1. Comments to Editor:**
The manuscript addresses a relevant research problem in explainable artificial intelligence and provides valuable insights into instance-level agreement between SHAP and LIME. The inclusion of within-method repeatability and instance-level reference comparisons strengthens its scientific contribution. However, several concerns require attention, particularly numerical inconsistencies in Tables 1, 4, and 5, training–test overlap, sensitivity to LIME configuration, and limitations in statistical generalizability.

**2. Comments to Author:**
The manuscript investigates an important issue in explainable artificial intelligence by examining instance-level agreement between SHAP and LIME. The experimental design, inclusion of within-method repeatability, and use of instance-level reference comparisons are valuable contributions. However, several issues should be addressed before publication. 1. Numerical Consistency: Tables 1, 4, and 5 contain inconsistencies in the signs of reported values and confidence interval bounds. For example, Table 4 reports positive differences where the displayed means indicate negative differences. All numerical results should be carefully verified against the original analysis outputs. 2. Training–Test Overlap: A substantial proportion of the Adult instances used in the analysis overlap with the model's training data. The authors should extend the held-out sensitivity analysis to the main agreement results. 3. LIME Configuration: The fixed kernel width and limited perturbation budget substantially influence the reported agreement. Additional sensitivity analyses using standard LIME settings and different sampling budgets are recommended. 4. Instance-Specific Agreement: The permutation reference should also be evaluated using instances from the same predicted class to better distinguish instance-specific agreement from class-dependent feature rankings. 5. Statistical Generalizability: The limited number of independent seeds and fitted models restricts the generalizability of the findings. The authors should strengthen the validation where feasible and maintain appropriately cautious interpretations. 6. Presentation and Reproducibility: The numerical inconsistencies should be corrected, and the agreement measures and experimental configurations should be clearly documented to facilitate independent verification.

**Comments and Suggestions for Author:** The manuscript presents an interesting and relevant investigation of agreement between SHAP and LIME explanations. The experimental approach and consideration of explanation repeatability are appreciated. However, substantial revisions are needed to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the findings. The authors should carefully verify the reported numerical results, address the methodological concerns regarding data partitioning and LIME parameter sensitivity, and clarify the limitations of the statistical analysis.

**Recommendation:** Major Revision

## Reviewer-3

**1. Comments to Editor:** The paper should be revised.

**2. Comments to Author:**
This manuscript investigates the extent to which SHAP and LIME agree when explaining the same prediction at the instance level. The topic is timely and relevant, addressing the increasingly recognized “disagreement problem” in explainable artificial intelligence. The study is well motivated, the research questions are clearly formulated, and the overall experimental framework is carefully designed. In particular, the use of paired explanations for the same instances, the pre-specified analysis plan, and the emphasis on reproducibility represent important strengths of the work. The manuscript is generally well written, technically rigorous, and transparent regarding both methodological decisions and study limitations. One of the most valuable contributions of the paper is that it moves beyond reporting agreement between SHAP and LIME and introduces meaningful reference points for interpreting the observed overlap. The inclusion of permutation-based baselines and self-agreement analyses provides additional context that is largely absent from previous studies. Furthermore, the discussion regarding the distinction between LIME coefficients and SHAP contributions, together with the sign-agreement analyses, offers an insightful methodological contribution that may benefit future comparative studies of explanation methods. Despite these strengths, several issues merit further consideration before publication. The primary concern relates to the configuration of LIME used throughout the study. The experiments employ a fixed kernel width of 3 and disable discretization of continuous features. The manuscript itself demonstrates that kernel width substantially influences the resulting explanations, with overlaps between explanations generated under different kernel-width settings being comparable to, or even larger than, the overlap observed between SHAP and LIME. Consequently, it remains unclear to what extent the reported disagreement reflects intrinsic differences between SHAP and LIME and to what extent it reflects the behavior of a particular, non-standard LIME configuration. While this limitation is acknowledged, a more extensive sensitivity analysis and a stronger justification for the parameter choice would significantly strengthen the validity of the conclusions. A second concern relates to the scope of the empirical evaluation. The study is based on three tabular datasets, which is sufficient for an initial investigation but limits the generalizability of the findings. The observed patterns may depend on dataset characteristics, including dimensionality, feature representation, or domain-specific properties. The discussion would benefit from a more explicit treatment of these generalization limits and a clearer distinction between evidence-supported conclusions and broader implications. The experimental design on the Adult dataset also deserves further discussion. Because the models are trained on a single partition while explanations are generated across multiple seeds, a substantial fraction of the explained instances belong to the training data. Although the author acknowledges this issue and performs additional analyses on held-out instances, the design introduces unnecessary complexity into the interpretation of the results. Training separate models for each seed would have provided a cleaner experimental setting and should at least be discussed as an alternative design choice. Another aspect that should be considered is the prominent role of post hoc analyses in the manuscript. Several of the most interesting findings, including the self-agreement experiments, permutation references, and the contribution-based sign analyses, were developed after inspection of the initial results. These analyses undoubtedly add value to the study; however, readers would benefit from a clearer separation between pre-specified and exploratory findings. The exploratory nature of these results should be emphasized more consistently throughout the manuscript. The quality of the writing is generally high. The presentation is clear, and the methodological details are reported thoroughly. The tables and figures effectively support the discussion. However, some sections, particularly the Discussion and Limitations sections, could be condensed without reducing their scientific value. At several points the manuscript revisits similar caveats, which affects readability and slightly weakens the impact of the main findings. Overall, I find the study scientifically meaningful and potentially publishable. The manuscript addresses an important question in explainable AI, employs a generally sound methodology, and offers several original contributions to the literature on explanation agreement. However, concerns regarding the chosen LIME configuration, the limited scope of the experimental evaluation, and the presentation of exploratory analyses should be addressed before the work can be considered for publication. Therefore, my recommendation is Major Revision. I believe the manuscript has strong publication potential if these issues are adequately addressed.

**Comments and Suggestions for Author:** I'd like to thank to author(s) for their valuable study.

**Recommendation:** Major Revision
